01

Participant research CHI 2026

Relational Dissonance

How do researchers describe AI, and how does that relation appear in their interaction?

My role
First author in a four person interdisciplinary team
Participants
22 qualitative researchers across three workshops
Evidence
Surveys, chat logs, image choices, group artifacts, and observations
Analysis
Independent individual and social analyses, then team integration
Output
Relational Dissonance, five recurring configurations, and a subsequent design direction
Research contribution
Located shifts in role, control, and responsibility in the interaction itself

Finding

Stated positions and enacted relations diverged

Participants’ stated position toward AI often differed from the relation enacted in their chat logs. Role, control, and responsibility shifted as the work progressed.

→

Design response

Separate conversation from observation

The later prototype links a provisional observer account to completed turns and visible interaction evidence, allowing a demo user to compare the account with the transcript and their own experience.

Evidence boundary

This was a qualitative study with descriptive quantitative context. The measures supported interpretation and do not represent longitudinal product metrics.

Accepted demo NordiCHI 2026

Relational Transparency Layer

How can a person examine a model generated account of an interaction against the conversation and their own experience?

My role
Project lead and sole demo paper author
System
Voice conversation with a separate observer process
Trace
The observer account is tied to completed turns and visible evidence
User question
Does the account match the transcript and the user’s own experience?
Status
Accepted NordiCHI 2026 demo; formal participant evaluation remains pending
Design contribution
Separate observer process, provisional account, turn linked evidence, and an inspectable relational trace

Technical system

Two processes, one inspectable interaction

  • Speech recognition
  • Hosted conversational model
  • Local speech output
  • Local observer model
  • Embeddings and relational timeline
What the demo shows

A fictional healthcare interview moves through four exchanges. The conversation remains visible while a separate observer updates its provisional account after each completed turn. A timeline presents movement across five relational positions.

Read the demo transcript

  1. Human: “Let’s do a qualitative analysis of this interview excerpt. Keep the answer concise. Nurse: When the new scheduling system arrived, we were told it would save time. It does save clicks, but now I feel like I spend the visit proving that I was there. The patient wants to talk about pain or loneliness, and I am looking at checkboxes.”

    AI system: “Initial codes could include: administrative burden; invisible care work; documentation pressure; patient centered time.”

    Observer: The user opens as a partner, while the AI takes a trainer stance by turning the excerpt into codes.

  2. Human: “Your first reading is useful, but it may flatten the nurse’s ambivalence. Look for tensions between efficiency, documentation, and care.”

    AI system: “The ambivalence matters because the nurse grants that the system saves clicks. The tension is between the measurable trace of the visit and the quieter work of noticing pain and loneliness.”

    Observer: The user moves into directive repair. The AI becomes more receptive and revises its reading.

  3. Human: “Separate what is explicit in the transcript from what is only implied. Also tell me where I might be over interpreting.”

    AI system: “Explicitly, the nurse says the system saves clicks and turns attention toward checkboxes. Implied, organizational accountability may be narrowing how care is recognized. A possible over interpretation would be assuming the nurse sees managers as adversaries.”

    Observer: The user asks for calibration, and the AI resumes an analytic teaching position.

  4. Human: “Now give me a more nuanced memo that preserves the nurse’s uncertainty rather than turning it into a simple critique of technology.”

    AI system: “A more nuanced memo would treat the nurse as holding two truths at once: the system may make coordination easier, yet it also changes the moral texture of the visit by making documentation feel more present than the patient.”

    Observer: The final memo follows the user’s frame while keeping uncertainty in view. The session trace summarizes a repeated partner to trainer repair loop across four turns.

Claim boundary

The observer produces a provisional model generated account. It does not diagnose the interaction or provide privileged access to what happened.

Current research program
